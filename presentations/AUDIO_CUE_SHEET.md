## How the sound works

* **All audio is embedded** as standard WAV inside the .pptx. There are no links, so it works offline. No video is used.
* **Two kinds of cue:**
  * *On slide start*: a music bed or sting that plays when you advance to the slide. It is timed to the Morph transition and to the animations that start on their own.
  * *With animations*: a short sound attached to a specific animation, so it fires on the same click, at the same moment.
* **You stay in control.** Nothing advances by itself. Every new step is your click. Automatic sequences are short (under 3 s), except the energy-flow loops, which are silent.
* **One sound at a time.** PowerPoint plays slide and animation sounds one at a time, so a new cue replaces the previous one. The deck relies on this deliberately: the night-time clock is cut by the instant trip, and the needle whine stops at the trip. Fast sequences such as stamps and chimes are pre-mixed into single files, so nothing is cut unintentionally.
* **Levels:** music beds sit about 6–8 dB below the sound effects, and both stay well below speech level. Set the room volume once on slide 1, then leave it.
* **Licensing:** every sound and every piece of music was synthesized from scratch for this deck. No third-party samples, so there are no licensing restrictions.

## Before the meeting (5-minute check)

1. Open the AUDIO file on the presentation laptop. Run slides 1, 11, 12, 17 and 22 in Slide Show mode and adjust the room volume.
2. Confirm the laptop audio goes to the room system, not the laptop speakers.
3. If anything sounds wrong on the day, switch to the SILENT file. It is the same deck with the same clicks and notes.
4. To cut sound mid-presentation, mute the laptop. The slides and clicks keep working exactly the same.
